## Tormenting Dash Revamp: Reading the New Movement

Tormenting Dash is the ability that defines NMI. It closes distance, it repositions you around obstacles, and it is the reason survivors have to respect open ground. | Situation | Recommended approach | Risk if you rush |
|---|---|---|
| Survivor is running a straight line | Commit to the dash early | Overshooting into a wall |
| Survivor is looping a tight structure | Hold the dash, bait the turn | Wasting the ability on a corner |

The practical takeaway: treat Tormenting Dash as a tool for closing a gap you have already created, not as a gap-opener. Good NMI players herd survivors toward dead ends and then dash. Great ones make the survivor *think* the dash is coming and force a bad turn first.

## Abyssal Escape NMI Tips: A Practice Roadmap

Learning a hunter is a matter of reps, not reading. Here is a session plan that works whether you have ten minutes or an hour.

- **Warm up on movement only.** Spend your first few matches dashing around the map without chasing anyone. Learn how far the dash carries you and how quickly you recover.
- **Practice the bait.** Approach a survivor, wait a beat, and see if they commit to a direction. Then dash. This is the single biggest skill gap between new and experienced NMI players.
- **Review your misses.** Every dash that hits a wall is information. Note the geometry that beat you.
- **Play survivor occasionally.** Nothing teaches you NMI's weaknesses faster than being chased by one.

| Session focus | Goal | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|
| Dash mechanics | Understand range and recovery | 10 minutes |
| Chase discipline | Bait before committing | 15 minutes |
| Map geometry | Learn the tight loops | 15 minutes |
| Match review | Identify wasted dashes | 10 minutes |

Per player experience, the fastest improvement comes from reducing wasted dashes rather than increasing aggressive ones. A hunter who dashes three times and hits twice is more dangerous than one who dashes eight times and hits twice.

## Survivor Counterplay: Facing the Abyssal Escape NMI

Survivors are not helpless against a completed NMI. The character's strength is commitment, and commitment is also the weakness: a hunter who has already spent the dash has to walk like everyone else.

| NMI action | Survivor response | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Charging a straight line | Break line of sight before the dash | Running predictably down open ground |
| Hovering near a loop | Hold the loop, do not panic-turn | Vaulting into the hunter's path |
| Dashing toward an objective | Rotate away and reset distance | Standing still to finish the interaction |
| Patrolling a cluster | Spread out so one dash cannot cover two players | Clustering on a single generator |

The general rule: make the hunter spend the dash on nothing. Every dash that misses is a window where the survivor controls the chase. Community reports consistently describe NMI as a character that feels overwhelming until you learn to bait the ability — then it becomes a mind game in your favor.
