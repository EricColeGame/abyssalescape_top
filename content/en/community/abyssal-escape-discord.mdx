### Where New Players Should Spend Their First Hour

Read the pinned messages in the channels that match your interests before posting. Pinned content is where communities archive answers to questions they are tired of answering — which means it is also where the fastest answers live. Then lurk in general chat for ten minutes to absorb the tone. Every server has one; matching it on day one saves you a lot of awkwardness.

### Where to Report Problems

Bug reports get acted on when they are specific. "It broke" tells a developer nothing. "It broke after I did X, on this platform, here is a screenshot" tells them almost everything. Community reports suggest that structured reports are far more likely to get a developer reply, simply because they are actionable.

## Etiquette That Keeps the Abyssal Escape Discord Useful

A community hub is only as good as the behavior inside it. These habits cost you nothing and make the server better for everyone.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Search before asking | Post the same question in four channels |
| Post feedback in the feedback channel | Bury suggestions in general chat |
| Share context, platform, and screenshots | Drop a one-word complaint and vanish |
| Respect spoiler tags for late-game content | Spoil story beats in open chat |
| Ask before DMing a developer | Treat staff DMs as a support desk |
| Credit creators when sharing clips | Repost someone's work without attribution |

- **Use threads for long discussions.** A twenty-message back-and-forth in general chat buries everything else.
- **Keep criticism specific and constructive.** "This section feels unfair because X" lands better than "this is bad."
- **Give time zones a thought.** A quiet channel at 3 a.m. your time is probably peak hours somewhere else.
- **Report, don't engage, with bad actors.** Moderators can act on reports; they cannot act on a flame war you joined.

## Staying Connected Without Drowning in Notifications

The fastest way to quit a server is to let it ping you 200 times a day. Set it up properly on day one:

1. Mute broad channels like general chat and off-topic.
2. Keep announcements and patch-notes channels unmuted.
3. Use server-specific notification overrides instead of muting the whole server.
4. Follow the threads you care about rather than the channels they live in.
5. Check in on a schedule — a quick daily scan beats endless lurking.

One more habit worth building: when you learn something useful — a fix, a route, a setting — write it up and post it. The members who contribute answers are the ones who end up recognized in the **Abyssal Escape Discord**, and recognition is often the first step toward getting early looks at what is coming next.
